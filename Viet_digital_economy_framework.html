Implementation Success Framework

A digital money system, built one careful step at a time

Vietnam is building a new system for digital assets and digital currency, made of six connected parts, run jointly by government and private partners. Each part must be tested and proven safe before the next one begins — that one rule holds the whole plan together.

↓ Article I — Governance & Coordination
Article I

Governance & Coordination

This plan involves several government bodies and private tech partners working side by side. Without one team clearly in charge, projects like this usually fall apart — unclear ownership is the number one reason large technology programs fail.

Global On-Chain Economy Alliance

GOE Alliance — VIFC-HCMC

Launched at the Vietnam International Financial Centre in Ho Chi Minh City, the GOE Alliance functions as the public-private convening layer sitting between the Strategic Steering Committee and individual technical or regulatory workstreams. It does not replace ministry authority — it operationalizes the strategy by aggregating private-sector capacity (1Matrix, FPT, Viettel, VBA members, international partners) into a single body accountable for sandbox delivery and cross-border interoperability, with VIFC as its physical and jurisdictional anchor.

Arm 01

Sandbox Delivery

Operates VASP / exchange cohorts through Decree 94/2025/ND-CP admission, standing up shared test environments and conformance suites so applicants aren't each re-solving interoperability from scratch.

Arm 02

International Connectivity

Represents Vietnam's on-chain infrastructure — VBSN, NDAChain, akaChain — in cross-border standards bodies and investor-facing forums, giving foreign regulators a single counterpart instead of ministry-by-ministry negotiation.

Article II

Phased Rollout Roadmap

Going in the right order matters more than going fast. Identity comes before markets. Markets come before the digital currency itself. Each stage needs a clear pass-or-fail check before the next one starts — click a stage below to see it.

  • Finalize the interoperability specification between VNeID (Level 2 eID), NDAChain (ZKP layer), and VBSN sub-chains.
  • Formally charter the GOE Alliance's mandate and reporting line into the Strategic Steering Committee, scoped to coordination — not supervision.
  • Publish Decree 94/2025/ND-CP sandbox admission criteria and select the first cohort; GOE Alliance operates the shared sandbox infrastructure.
  • Lock the cryptographic standards baseline (Government Cipher Committee) before any production deployment.
  • NDAChain ZK-proof verification operational for a limited institutional eKYC pilot.
  • VNeID biometric linkage tested with 2–3 commercial banks under Project 06 before national rollout.
  • Success gate: false-rejection/false-acceptance rates on biometric matching published and independently audited.
  • First licensed exchanges onboarded under SSC/UBCKNN with mandatory VNeID integration.
  • VBSN sector sub-chains activated for RWA pilots only — not general-purpose trading — to contain blast radius.
  • Real-time surveillance feed (SSC + A05) validated against live transaction volume before scaling exchange count.
  • akaChain (FPT) and eTradevn scaled for supply-chain finance and e-Bills of Lading.
  • VIFC cross-border bridging tested against a small set of ISO 20022 / W3C DID counterpart jurisdictions before general availability.
  • GOE Alliance formally represents Vietnam's chain infrastructure in bilateral/multilateral standards talks, each agreement logged against a common interoperability checklist.
  • Viettel VMB node network stress-tested for offline wallet resilience under adverse network conditions.
  • Atomic DvP settlement (tokenized assets against CBDC) piloted in a single sector — land registry or trade finance — before generalization.
  • Contingent on Phases 0–3 passing independent audit — not a fixed calendar date.
Design principle

No stage starts until an independent check on the last one is finished and published. This matters most for the digital currency stage — it should never launch on a fixed date. It should launch only once the earlier stages have passed their checks, because a failure in a national currency system hurts everyone far more than a failure in a market system.

Article III

What It Takes to Succeed, Part by Part

Each part of the system can fail in its own way. Naming each risk clearly — instead of lumping everything under one vague label — is what makes it possible to actually manage.

01

Trust Infrastructure

Risk

NDAChain and VBSN are built by different consortiums (MPS/MIC vs. 1Matrix/BCG/VBA) on different technical stacks — interoperability isn't guaranteed by strategy documents alone.

Mitigation

A shared interoperability test suite with published conformance results before either chain is certified for production. Treat this as technical, not political.

Success factor

A single canonical DID resolution path — ambiguity here fragments trust across the ecosystem.

02

Legal & Regulatory Framework

Risk

Sandbox rules (Decree 94/2025/ND-CP) and 2026 MoF circulars evolving concurrently with technical build-out creates a moving compliance target.

Mitigation

Freeze regulatory requirements per sandbox cohort at admission date; apply new rules only to subsequent cohorts.

Success factor

Token classification (utility / security / RWA-backed) published before exchange licensing, not concurrent with it.

03

Market Infrastructure

Risk

Concentration risk — 1Matrix (VBSN) and FPT (akaChain) becoming de facto monopolies increases systemic fragility.

Mitigation

Keep licensing competitive against shared technical standards, rather than hard-coding two vendors into the architecture.

Success factor

Surveillance feeds (SSC/A05) tested for latency under peak load, not just steady state.

Risk — GOE Alliance

As the body closest to the vendors it coordinates, GOE Alliance risks becoming a vector for the same concentration risk it should mitigate without a convening/procurement firewall.

04

Investor Protection & Supervision

Risk

Automated fraud detection (NDAChain + VNeID) creates a single point of surveillance failure and civil-liberties exposure if biometric data is compromised.

Mitigation

Independent, recurring penetration testing of the VNeID-NDAChain link; statutory limits on secondary use of biometric data.

Success factor

A public incident-disclosure protocol for audit failures — silent patching erodes trust faster than disclosed incidents.

05

Human Capital

Risk

A four-tier education model spanning ~17M public down to compliance officers is historically under-resourced in execution.

Mitigation

Tie Tier 3/4 certification completion to exchange licensing eligibility — a market-driven incentive independent of campaign budgets.

Success factor

Track Tier 1 awareness by measured wallet-security incident rates, not campaign reach.

06

CBDC & Sovereign Settlement

Risk

Telco-dependent node infrastructure (Viettel VMB) is a single point of failure atypical for central bank infrastructure.

Mitigation

Require redundant node operators before CBDC settlement moves beyond pilot; define an SBV-controlled fallback path.

Success factor

Offline wallet protocol tested under actual network-outage conditions, not simulated.

Article IV

How the Parts Depend on Each Other

Identity leads to markets, and markets lead to settlement — so a delay anywhere upstream slows everything downstream. Here are five chains worth watching closely.

VNeID Level 2 eID→ NDAChain ZKP verification→ Exchange eKYC→ Licensed trading A delay in national eID rollout directly blocks exchange licensing timelines.
Cipher Committee standards→ All chain deployments Standards must be finalized before, not alongside, VBSN/NDAChain production deployment to avoid costly retrofits.
SSC sandbox cohort outcomes→ Subsequent regulatory circulars Regulation should be empirically informed by sandbox results, not finalized in parallel with the first cohort's operation.
VMB node stress-test results→ CBDC settlement go-live The dependency most likely to be politically pressured into premature go-live — this gate should be independently auditable and resistant to calendar override.
GOE Alliance charter & mandate→ Sandbox velocity + cross-border credibility An unchartered GOE Alliance stalls; an over-scoped one overreaches into de facto regulation. Treat its Phase 0 charter as a program dependency, same footing as the cryptographic baseline.

Article VII

Roadmap — Closing the Gaps

The plan above lays out what's already been designed. But it doesn't yet answer some big questions people will ask in year one — what happens if a stage fails its check, who pays for shared systems, and how does someone get their money back if something breaks. These gaps are worth closing before they turn into real incidents.

Shortcomings not yet covered by the framework

01

No funding or cost-recovery model

Nothing specifies who pays for GOE Alliance operations, shared sandbox infrastructure, or recurring audits — ministry budget, cohort fees, or licensing levies.

02

No phase-failure protocol

"No phase begins until the prior audit passes" is a gate, not a plan — there's no defined remediation window, partial-rollback procedure, or disclosure requirement for what happens when an audit fails.

03

No consumer redress channel

Pillar 4 covers detection and surveillance of abuse, but not a route for an ordinary user to recover frozen funds or dispute a transaction — enforcement isn't redress.

04

No formal biometric data-sharing MOU

MPS holds VNeID biometric data; SBV and SSC need it for AML/eKYC. The framework assumes this link exists without naming the legal instrument — Decree 13/2023/ND-CP on personal data protection — that should govern its scope and retention.

05

No second vendor bench

The concentration-risk mitigation says "keep licensing competitive," but no concrete step exists to qualify a second Layer-1 or BaaS provider before the ecosystem is architecturally dependent on 1Matrix and FPT.

06

No GOE Alliance sunset or renewal clause

A chartered mandate without a review date tends to persist and expand by default rather than by decision.

07

No reserve or insurance fund

Nothing capitalizes a backstop for smart-contract failure or RWA reserve shortfall — the framework audits for these events but doesn't fund recovery from them.

08

No talent-retention strategy

Tier 3/4 certification builds exactly the skilled workforce international employers will recruit; the human capital pillar has no plan to retain them.

Immediate — 0 to 6 months

Do alongside Phase 0

  1. Publish a cost-recovery model for GOE Alliance operations and shared sandbox infrastructure before the Phase 0 charter is finalized — licensing fees and cohort fees, not open-ended ministry budget.Owner: GOE Alliance + MoF
  2. Define the phase-failure protocol in writing: remediation window, conditions for partial rollback of already-deployed components, and a public disclosure requirement.Owner: Strategic Steering Cmte
  3. Draft the MPS–SBV–SSC biometric data MOU, scoped against Decree 13/2023/ND-CP, before Phase 1's biometric bank pilot goes live.Owner: MPS + SBV + SSC
  4. Open a second Layer-1/BaaS qualification track in parallel with 1Matrix/FPT deployment, so an alternative is architecturally viable before Phase 2 scales exchange count.Owner: Technical WG
  5. Stand up an independent consumer ombudsman channel, separate from SSC enforcement, for frozen-fund and disputed-transaction complaints.Owner: SSC + MoF
Long-term — 12 to 36+ months

Build through Phases 2–4

  1. Legislate a 3-year sunset/renewal clause for the GOE Alliance charter, reviewed against the same audit evidence as the phase gates.Owner: Steering Cmte
  2. Capitalize a cybersecurity and reserve insurance fund, sized against RWA custody exposure and funded from exchange licensing fees.Owner: SSC + Licensed Exchanges
  3. Extend the interoperability conformance suite beyond NDAChain/VBSN to legacy banking core systems, ahead of Phase 3 enterprise scaling.Owner: Technical WG + Banks
  4. Build a talent-retention program — compensation benchmarking and secondment pathways — for Tier 3/4 certified developers and compliance officers.Owner: ABAII + SSC Training Ctr

Reading this roadmap

None of these items need to happen before Stage 0 can start — but a few of them (the funding plan, the rollback plan, the data-sharing agreement) should count as Stage 0 work in their own right, just as important as the security standards. Putting them off doesn't make the risk go away — it just makes the problem more expensive to fix later.

"Good systems are built the same way good things are always built — one careful step at a time, each one checked before the next begins. Nothing grows bigger until what's underneath it is solid."

This plan brings order and sequencing to the six-part system already agreed on. It doesn't change who's responsible for what — only the order they work in and the checks along the way. The GOE Alliance's job is to bring people together, not to regulate; its role should stay clearly defined, not assumed.
