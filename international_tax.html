The Law & Application to the Facts

01

Sale of Australian Shares by Selznick

Low Risk · CGT Exempt
▾

Applicable Law — Division 855 & Taxable Australian Property

Under Division 855, ITAA 1997, a foreign resident can disregard a capital gain or loss from a CGT event if the relevant asset is not 'Taxable Australian Property' (TAP). Section 855-10 provides that foreign residents are not subject to Australian CGT on non-TAP assets.

For shares in an Australian company to constitute TAP as an indirect Australian real property interest under s 855-25, two cumulative tests must both be satisfied:

Non-portfolio interest test: The taxpayer and its associates (under s 318, ITAA 1936) must hold, in aggregate, a 10% or more associate-inclusive direct participation interest in the entity.

Principal asset test (s 855-30): The market value of the entity's assets must be principally attributable (more than 50%) to Australian real property.

Application to Facts

Selznick holds a 15% interest in the Australian software company with no voting rights. The non-portfolio interest test may technically be met (15% ≥ 10%), but must consider the associate-inclusive test carefully — if Selznick's associates hold no additional interests, the result remains to be confirmed.

Critically, the principal asset test plainly fails: the facts expressly state the software company has no real property in Australia. The shares therefore do not constitute an indirect Australian real property interest, are not TAP, and Selznick is entitled to disregard any capital gain under s 855-10.

✅
No Australian CGT The shares are not TAP — the principal asset test is not satisfied (no Australian real property). No FRCGW withholding obligation arises on the purchaser. Selznick should maintain documentation confirming the software company's asset composition in the event of ATO review.

Additional Considerations

FRCGW regime: If CGT were to apply (e.g. additional associate holdings triggered the non-portfolio interest test), the foreign resident capital gains withholding (FRCGW) regime would apply. Following amendments effective 1 January 2025, the FRCGW rate is 15% with no minimum transaction value threshold. Selznick would need to obtain a variation notice from the ATO.

CGT Discount: As a foreign resident company, Selznick would not be entitled to the CGT discount under s 115-10. However, France may provide a foreign tax credit for Australian CGT paid under Article 23 of the Australia-France treaty.

Treaty position: Under Article 13 of the Australia-France treaty, Australia retains the right to tax gains from alienation of shares deriving more than 50% of their value from immovable property in Australia. As the software company has no Australian real property, this article grants Australia no taxing rights — consistent with the domestic law analysis.

Opinion — Issue 1

No Australian CGT applies to Selznick's sale of shares in the Australian software company. The shares are not TAP as the principal asset test is not satisfied (no Australian real property). The sale proceeds are not assessable in Australia. No FRCGW withholding obligation arises on the purchaser.

Selznick Dividend to Futuro — CFC Analysis & Choices

Medium Risk · Planning Opportunity
▾

A. Is Selznick a Controlled Foreign Company (CFC)?

Futuro holds a 60% interest in Selznick. Under s 340, ITAA 1936, a company is a CFC if Australian residents have, in aggregate, an associate-inclusive control interest of greater than 50%. Futuro's 60% holding (as an attributable taxpayer under s 361) plainly satisfies this test. Selznick is a CFC.

France is a 'listed country' for CFC purposes under s 320 and the relevant ITAA 1936 regulations (updated 2025). Futuro's attribution interest is 60% per s 362.

B. Active Income Test

The CFC rules only require attribution of 'tainted income' to Futuro where Selznick fails the active income test (AIT) under s 432. The AIT is failed where the ratio of tainted income to total income exceeds 5%.

Selznick is described as an active AI research company deriving income from data analytics activities. On this basis, there are strong grounds to conclude the AIT is satisfied (not failed), meaning no CFC attribution of income has occurred historically.

⚠️
Confirm Active Income Test Insufficient information exists to state this categorically. Futuro must confirm that Selznick's data analytics activities constitute active business income and that the tainted income ratio does not exceed 5%. The distinction between passive royalties and genuinely active income is critical.

D. French WHT — Australia-France DTA

French domestic law would ordinarily impose WHT on dividends paid to a non-resident. Under Article 10(2)(a) of the Australia-France DTA, the WHT rate is reduced to 5% where the recipient holds ≥10% of the voting power, subject to the MLI 365-day holding period condition (Article 8, MLI). Futuro holds 60% of Selznick.

Critical issue: The facts state that Selznick's shares carry no voting power. If Futuro's 60% holding carries no voting rights, the reduced 5% rate may not be available and the standard 15% rate under Article 10(2)(b) would apply. This must be confirmed by French advisers.

FITO availability: No FITO under Division 770 is available for the French WHT — whether the dividend is NANE under Subdiv 768-A or exempt under s 23AI, no Australian income tax arises on the dividend. The French WHT is an irrecoverable cost.

E. Conduit Foreign Income (CFI) — Group Planning

The group should elect the CFI rules under Division 802, ITAA 1997 to allow tax-free pass-through of foreign income to non-resident shareholders. All of Futuro's shareholders are either directly or indirectly non-residents (Blighty is UK resident; Snags is 100% owned by a Chinese investor). The CFI rules are highly relevant.

CFI income can include NANE dividends under Subdiv 768-A. Where Futuro and Snags designate unfranked dividends as CFI, those dividends are exempt from Australian WHT when paid to non-resident shareholders. Declarations must be made at or before the time of distribution. CFI tracking accounts must be reduced under s 802-45 when CFI distributions are made.

Hecht Residency Change — Domestic & Treaty Considerations

High Risk · Urgent Action
▾
🚨
Immediate Action Required — MAP Process The management changes at Hecht are critical. The MLI MAP process must be initiated with the ATO immediately. Until a competent authority agreement is reached, Hecht risks dual taxation in both Australia and Poland without treaty relief.

A. Historical Position — Hecht as Australian Resident

Hecht is incorporated in Poland but has historically been managed by Australian resident directors through central management and control (CMAC) located in Australia. Under s 6(1), ITAA 1936, a company whose CMAC is in Australia is an Australian tax resident. As a dual resident (also Polish tax resident by incorporation), the Australia-Poland DTA tiebreaker had applied, with effective management in Australia determining Australian residency.

B. MLI Modification — Mutual Agreement Process

The Australia-Poland DTA is a covered agreement for MLI purposes. Article 4 of the MLI has replaced the prior automatic tiebreaker rule (based on place of effective management) with a mutual agreement process (MAP). The ATO and Polish tax authorities must now agree on the country of residence for treaty purposes.

Given the significant shift of management activities to Poland, Hecht may no longer have its effective management in Australia. Futuro must initiate the competent authority MAP process with the ATO as soon as possible. The OECD released an updated MAP Manual in November 2025 providing detailed guidance.

C. Consequences of Dual Residency Without MAP Agreement

In the absence of a competent authority agreement, Hecht will be treated as tax resident in both Australia and Poland. No treaty benefits will be available to resolve the conflict. Both jurisdictions could seek to tax Hecht's entire income, and no treaty-based credit relief would be available — creating significant double taxation risk.

Does Blighty Have a Permanent Establishment in Australia?

High Risk · PE Confirmed
▾

Applicable Law — Article 5, Australia-UK DTA

Whether Blighty has an Australian PE must be determined under Article 5 of the Australia-UK DTA. The general rule is that a PE exists where there is a fixed place of business through which the business of the enterprise is wholly or partly carried on. TR 2002/5 confirms that a PE requires: (i) a place of business; (ii) that is 'fixed' (geographic and temporal permanence); and (iii) through which business is carried on.

PE Confirmed — Queensland Manufacturing Facility & Offices Blighty will have at least one PE in Australia. Both the manufacturing facility and the leased offices satisfy geographic and temporal permanence (TR 2002/5, paras 9, 17–19). The activities — manufacturing hardware and conducting sales — are the core business and cannot be characterised as preparatory or auxiliary. The Art 5(4) exception does not apply.

Single vs Multiple PEs

The manufacturing facility and multiple offices may constitute a single cohesive PE or multiple PEs. Para 22 of the 2017 OECD Commentary on Article 5 addresses this: where there is sufficient geographic and commercial coherence between separate places of business, they may constitute a single PE. As all Australian activities relate to Blighty's manufacturing and global sales operations, there is a strong argument for a single integrated PE.

Dependent Agent PE Risk

While all sales contract approvals are finalised in the UK, Article 5(5) provides that a deemed PE exists where a person in Australia habitually exercises authority to conclude contracts on Blighty's behalf. Even if Australian staff are involved in negotiating and effectively concluding contracts (with formal sign-off in the UK), the OECD Commentary (para 88) suggests this may be sufficient. This risk must be carefully monitored and Australian staff authority tightly defined.

Opinion — Issue 4

Blighty will have a PE in Australia by reason of both the Queensland manufacturing facility and the leased sales offices. These constitute fixed places of business with geographic and temporal permanence through which Blighty's business is carried on. The preparatory/auxiliary exceptions do not apply. A dependent agent PE may also arise depending on the role of Australian staff in the sales process. Blighty should establish governance arrangements to limit PE scope, particularly around contract approval authority.

Australian Income Tax Implications of the Blighty PE

High Risk · Compliance Critical
▾

A. Attribution of Business Profits — Article 7

Article 7 of the Australia-UK Convention gives Australia the right to tax business profits attributable to the PE. Article 7(2) requires profits to be determined as if the PE were a distinct and independent enterprise dealing at arm's length with Blighty. Under Division 764, ITAA 1997, income from the PE is treated as Australian-sourced.

As the hardware is manufactured in Australia, sold from Australian offices, and income flows through an Australian bank account, a significant portion of the income will be Australian-sourced and attributable to the PE — consistent with Lewis Berger & Sons (source of manufacturing income partly in the place of manufacture).

This position was confirmed in Max Factor & Co v FCT (1984) 84 ATC 4060 and TR 2001/11 / ATO ID 2012/90.

C. Withholding Tax on Interest Paid by PE to Blighty

When the PE remits interest to Blighty (a non-resident), s 128B(2)(b)(ii), ITAA 1936 imposes Australian interest WHT. This is reduced to 10% under Article 11(2) of the Australia-UK Convention. If WHT is not withheld and remitted, the corresponding interest deduction will be denied under s 26-25, ITAA 1997.

D. Repatriation of PE Profits & CGT on PE Assets
