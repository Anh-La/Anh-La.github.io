01

Residency of UA — Overseas Board of Directors

High Risk

Applicable Law

ITAA 1936 s 6(1) AUS-Canada DTA Art 4 MLI (tie-breaker) TR 2018/5 PCG 2018/9

Under s 6(1) ITAA 1936, a company is an Australian resident if it is incorporated in Australia, or if it carries on business in Australia and either has its central management and control (CMAC) in Australia or its voting power is controlled by Australian residents. UA's Australian incorporation alone is sufficient under the first limb — regardless of where management is exercised.

⚠️
Dual Residency Risk UA is wholly owned by Universal Inc (Canada). Canadian domestic law may also treat UA as a Canadian resident, triggering potential dual residency governed by Article 4 of the AUS-Canada DTA. Post-MLI modification, the automatic "place of effective management" tie-breaker has been replaced by a Mutual Agreement Procedure (MAP) — creating outcome uncertainty if no agreement is reached.

CMAC Analysis

Under TR 2018/5, CMAC is determined by where high-level strategic decisions are actually made. UA's proposed board — 3 US directors, 1 Canadian, 1 UK director, NO Australian directors — strongly indicates that substantive decision-making will occur entirely outside Australia, heightening the dual residency risk.

Recommendations

1
Appoint an Australian-based director with genuine authority to participate in high-level decisions — immediately, before incorporation of UA.
2
Hold substantive board meetings in Australia or ensure key decisions are formally made or ratified in Australia, supported by clear minutes.
3
Implement governance documentation (board charter, decision protocols) aligned with TR 2018/5 and PCG 2018/9.
4
Monitor the MAP process under the AUS-Canada DTA and seek to establish CMAC in Australia before any dual-residency competent authority engagement is triggered.

Employee PE Risk in Overseas Jurisdictions

High Risk

Applicable Law

OECD Model Art 5 OECD Art 5(5) (Dependent Agent) BEPS Action 7 TR 2002/5 PCG 2023/1

A permanent establishment generally arises where an enterprise has a fixed place of business through which its business is carried on. Under OECD Model Art 5 and TR 2002/5, three elements are required: (1) a physical place of business, (2) sufficient permanence, and (3) the business is conducted through that location.

Under the updated OECD Commentary, remote working arrangements that are regular, recurring, or indefinite — rather than temporarily exceptional (e.g., COVID-19) — are more likely to satisfy the permanence requirement. PCG 2023/1 aligns the ATO's position with this view.

Under Art 5(5) (as amended by BEPS Action 7), a dependent agent PE arises where employees habitually play the principal role leading to contract conclusion — even without formally signing contracts. This creates particular risk for marketing employees actively soliciting and negotiating agreements.

⚠️
Material PE Risk — NZ and South-East Asia Employees working remotely for up to 8 months or indefinitely, and marketing staff actively concluding customer agreements, face a heightened PE creation risk. Each jurisdiction's specific DTA must be assessed alongside local domestic law.

Indonesian Data Centre Servers — Fixed Place of Business PE

High Risk

Applicable Law

OECD Model Art 5 Commentary OECD Server PE Guidance AUS-Indonesia DTA

Under the OECD Commentary on Art 5, a server can constitute a PE where it is physically located in a jurisdiction, has sufficient geographic and temporal permanence, and is used to carry on the core business activities of the enterprise. A distinction is drawn between preparatory/auxiliary functions (e.g., data storage, system support) and substantive business operations.

🚨
Strong Likelihood of Indonesian PE NovaTech's Indonesian servers: (1) have been physically located there for ~2 years — permanence satisfied; (2) perform automated core business functions (sales to Indonesian customers + payment processing) — not merely preparatory or auxiliary; (3) operations are carried on through the servers located in Indonesia. This falls squarely within the OECD definition of a fixed-place PE.

Recommendations

1
Seek local Indonesian tax advice immediately to determine whether PE registration and tax filing obligations exist and quantify liabilities from prior periods.
2
Consider migrating to a cloud-based model (with no fixed physical server in Indonesia) to reduce future PE risk.
3
Disclose this issue clearly in due diligence documentation; incorporate appropriate representations, warranties, and indemnities in the share purchase agreement (SPA).
4
Quantify any potential purchase price adjustment to reflect Indonesian tax liabilities.

Latvian Royalty Payment — WHT Shortfall

High Risk · Immediate Action

Applicable Law

ITAA 1936 s 128B ITAA 1936 s 128D ITAA 1997 s 26-25 TR 2024/D1 FCT v PepsiCo [2025] HCA 30

Under s 128B ITAA 1936, WHT applies to royalties paid by an Australian resident to a non-resident. The tax is a final tax under s 128D. Where no DTA applies, the default rate is 30% of the gross royalty. Australia has no DTA with Latvia — the 5% rate applied (available only to French DTA residents) was incorrect.

The High Court in FCT v PepsiCo Inc [2025] HCA 30 confirmed that the royalty definition is interpreted broadly; WHT obligations apply based on the legal character of the payment. TR 2024/D1 provides guidance on software, IP, and licensing royalty characterisation.

Under s 26-25 ITAA 1997, a deduction for royalty payments is denied to the extent WHT has not been correctly withheld and remitted — creating additional tax exposure.

Belgian Share Sale — CGT Treatment in FY25

High Risk · Amend Return

Applicable Law

ITAA 1997 Div 855 ITAA 1997 Subdiv 768-G ITAA 1997 CGT event A1 ITAA 1997 s 50% CGT Discount

Division 855 disregards gains on taxable Australian property for foreign residents. NovaTech is an Australian resident — Div 855 has no application here. The exclusion of the A$10m gain was incorrect.

Subdivision 768-G allows an Australian company to disregard gains on foreign company shares where the taxpayer holds at least a 10% participation interest. NovaTech held only 9% — below the statutory threshold. This exemption is unavailable.

As NovaTech is a company, it is not eligible for the 50% CGT discount. The full A$10m gain is therefore assessable in FY25.

🚨
Reliance on ChatGPT for Tax Advice The exclusion of the A$10m capital gain was based on a ChatGPT analysis — not professional tax advice. This is an incorrect and potentially negligent approach that has resulted in a material understatement of taxable income. An amended return must be lodged.

India Transfer Pricing — MAP & Double Taxation

Medium Risk · Time-Sensitive

Applicable Law

ITAA 1997 Div 815 AUS-India DTA Art 25 MLI Art 17 Oracle Corporation [2025] FCAFC 145

NovaTech accepted an ATO transfer pricing primary adjustment under Division 815. The Indian tax authorities have refused to make a corresponding downward adjustment, resulting in economic double taxation — the same income taxed in both Australia and India.

The AUS-India DTA provides a Mutual Agreement Procedure (MAP) under Article 25. MLI Article 17 requires treaty partners to make corresponding adjustments to eliminate double taxation from TP adjustments. In Oracle Corporation [2025] FCAFC 145, the Full Federal Court confirmed that Australian court proceedings may be stayed while MAP is ongoing — providing procedural relief.

⏳
Time Limit — Act Immediately A MAP request must generally be lodged with the ATO within three years of the initial adjustment. Missing this deadline may permanently extinguish treaty relief and leave NovaTech bearing the full double-tax burden.

Recommendations

1
Lodge a MAP request with the ATO immediately under Art 25 (AUS-India DTA) to initiate bilateral negotiations with the Indian competent authority.
2
Consider seeking a stay of domestic recovery action in Australia while MAP is ongoing, as supported by Oracle Corporation [2025].
3
For ongoing related-party transactions, consider a bilateral Advance Pricing Arrangement (APA) with both ATO and Indian authorities to prevent future disputes.
4
Disclose MAP risk and cost/uncertainty to Universal in due diligence; factor into transaction pricing or indemnity provisions.

IP Transfer to Canada — Australian Tax Implications

Medium Risk · Structural Planning

Applicable Law

ITAA 1997 CGT event A1 ITAA 1997 Div 815 (TP) PCG 2024/1 (DEMPE) TR 2024/D1 (Royalties) AUS-Canada DTA (5% WHT) R&D Tax Incentive

The IP transfer will trigger CGT event A1. For internally developed IP, the cost base may be nil or low (development costs already deducted), generating a substantial taxable gain. As a company, NovaTech has no access to the 50% CGT discount. The transfer must occur at arm's length value under Division 815 — undervaluation will attract ATO adjustment.

The ATO will scrutinise the arrangement under PCG 2024/1, focusing on whether DEMPE functions (Development, Enhancement, Maintenance, Protection, Exploitation) have genuinely shifted to Canada, or whether NovaTech retains the economic substance while Canada merely holds the legal title.

Post-transfer royalties paid to Canada will require correct characterisation under TR 2024/D1. The AUS-Canada DTA reduces royalty WHT to 5% — failure to correctly withhold and remit would deny NovaTech the royalty deduction under s 26-25.

If NovaTech operates as an R&D service centre for Canada, it may lose eligibility for the Australian R&D tax incentive, which requires that R&D be conducted for the taxpayer's own benefit.

🔬
ATO Audit Risk — IP Migration The ATO has flagged IP migration as a high-priority audit target under PCG 2024/1. If NovaTech retains DEMPE substance in Australia while IP legal ownership shifts to Canada, the ATO is likely to challenge the arm's length pricing and profit allocation.

Recommendations

1
Obtain independent IP valuation to support the transfer price and document the arm's-length basis robustly.
2
Align DEMPE functions with legal ownership in Canada — Canadian personnel must genuinely direct development, enhancement, and exploitation decisions.
3
Carefully structure intercompany R&D arrangements so NovaTech retains an economic interest in R&D outcomes (e.g., co-development, enhanced rights), preserving R&D tax incentive eligibility.
4
Correctly withhold 5% WHT on royalty payments to Canada under the AUS-Canada DTA and document treaty eligibility.
5
Consider a staged or alternative migration approach to manage the timing and magnitude of the upfront CGT liability.

Pre-Sale Partially Franked Dividend — WHT Implications

Medium Risk · Planning Opportunity

Applicable Law

ITAA 1997 Div 7A ITAA 1997 Div 974 (Debt/Equity) ITAA 1997 CFI Rules AUS-UK DTA (5% WHT) AUS-Ireland DTA

For Australian resident shareholders (Povey Industries Ltd and Heath Sheeran), dividends are assessable income with a gross-up for attached franking credits — franking credits offset income tax liabilities.

For non-resident shareholders, WHT applies to the unfranked portion only — the franked portion is exempt from WHT. Applicable rates:
— Emerald Ltd (UK): 5% WHT on unfranked portion under AUS-UK DTA.
— Ben Purvis (Irish resident): reduced treaty rate under AUS-Ireland DTA.

Under Division 974, any instruments classified as debt rather than equity may cause payments to be treated as interest instead of dividends — with different WHT consequences.

The conduit foreign income (CFI) rules may allow certain unfranked dividends sourced from foreign income to be paid to non-residents free of WHT, materially improving after-tax outcomes for offshore shareholders.

Post-Acquisition Profit Repatriation — NovaTech to Universal Inc

Planning Opportunity

Applicable Law

ITAA 1997 Part 3-90 (Consolidation) ITAA 1997 CFI Rules ITAA 1997 s 25-90 AUS-Canada DTA (5% WHT) TD 2020/7 (FITO & CG)

Tax Consolidation (Part 3-90): UA and NovaTech should form an Australian tax consolidated group immediately post-acquisition. Benefits include: single tax return for the group, pooled franking account, tax cost setting rules resetting NovaTech's asset costs to reflect the acquisition price (reducing future CGT exposure), and no tax consequences on intra-group transactions.

Intra-group dividends (NovaTech → UA): Both are Australian residents — no WHT applies. Dividends can be franked using the consolidated franking account.

Outbound dividends (UA → Universal Inc, Canada): Under the AUS-Canada DTA, WHT on unfranked dividends is reduced to 5% (subject to ownership requirements). Franked dividends are exempt from WHT. CFI can eliminate WHT on qualifying foreign-source income components.

Interest deductibility (s 25-90): Where borrowing funds generate NANE income (e.g., foreign dividends), interest deductions may be partially denied — careful financing modelling required.

TD 2020/7 limits foreign income tax offsets (FITOs) in respect of capital gains — relevant where NovaTech derives assessable foreign-source capital gains.

✅
Planning Opportunity — Tax Consolidated Group Forming the consolidated group immediately post-acquisition maximises efficiency and minimises WHT leakage. The sooner consolidation is elected, the sooner the group benefits from reset asset costs, pooled franking credits, and streamlined compliance.

Appendix — Code of Professional Conduct (TASA 2009)

Professional Standards
⚠️
Serious Concern — Existing Tax Agent Conduct The current tax agent's advice to the CFO not to disclose the Latvian WHT shortfall raises serious breaches under the Tax Agent Services Act 2009 (TASA) Code of Professional Conduct.

Applicable Law

Tax Agent Services Act 2009 TASA Code of Professional Conduct 2024 Professional Standards Update Tax Practitioners Board (TPB)

The TASA Code requires tax practitioners to: act with honesty and integrity, comply with taxation laws, provide clients with appropriate advice on their obligations, and ensure clients are informed of their rights. The 2024 updated professional standards expressly prohibit advising clients to adopt positions that result in false or misleading statements to tax authorities.

Advising concealment of a known tax error: (a) reflects a lack of professional integrity; (b) breaches the duty of care and competence; (c) may constitute obstruction of the proper administration of the tax system — one of the more serious TASA breaches.

Serious breaches may trigger mandatory reporting obligations to the TPB. Sanctions can include formal cautions, enforced training, suspension, or termination of registration. Further legal consequences may arise in severe cases.

Identified Breaches

  • Advised client to conceal a known WHT shortfall — inconsistent with honesty and integrity obligations.
  • Failed to advise the client of their obligation to correct the error and the benefits of voluntary disclosure.
  • May have been involved in preparing tax filings while aware of the incorrect WHT rate — compounding the breach.
  • Advising non-disclosure constitutes a breach of the duty to support proper tax administration.

Recommendation

Universal should discontinue engagement with the current tax agent following the acquisition and appoint a new adviser with appropriate governance and compliance standards. If the agent was knowingly involved in the incorrect filing, a referral to the TPB for investigation should be considered.
