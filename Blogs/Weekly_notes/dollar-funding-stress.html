Where it's playing out

United States

The 19 August buyback ceiling was raised to $4bn; the 30-year fell roughly 9bp and gave nearly all of it back within the week (Johnson, 23 Aug; Gammon, 27 Aug). This is not QE — it's a reverse auction, debt is retired, and no new money is created. The next tool under discussion is a Treasury General Account (TGA) drawdown of up to roughly $500bn — cash already earmarked for entitlement spending — used to buy 10-year notes, with issuance later refilling the account.

Japan / Asia

Steve Hanke's 18 August read: yen intervention fails the same test it failed in 1997. NIIP dynamics and the carry trade remain the binding constraint, not the spot-market intervention itself — a pattern Snider separately frames as an "eurodollar thermometer" reading on the same underlying stress.

Global

Michael Howell's 22 July view sets the ceiling on the whole thread: global liquidity has already peaked, a 5–6 year refinancing wall is approaching, and ultimately the market — not any single buyback program — sets the long end if the official bid isn't large enough to matter.

How the analysts read it

Brent Johnson

The dollar as a post-1971 "dagger"; the Triffin flip is real, but the debasement trade is not a straight line — shortage episodes tend to strengthen the dollar first, before any structural decline shows up.

Michael Howell

Global liquidity already peaked. The 5–6 year refinancing wall is the structural clock underneath everything else in this thread. The market sets the rate if the official bid is too small — full stop.

Steve Hanke

Yen intervention fails the 1997 test. Spot-market defense doesn't fix an NIIP and carry-trade problem.

Jeff Snyder (Eurodollar University)

Four distinct growth/inflation regimes exist, and a default 60/40 portfolio fails specifically in the wrong one — which regime you're in matters more than any single indicator.

Jeff Snider

Nobody can count the relevant money. M1 and M2 are "obsolete domestic fossils" — eurodollar instruments, swaps, and derivatives behave as money in ways the official stock measures simply don't see. Fed "printing," in this read, is often a reaction to unseen destruction elsewhere in the system, not a proactive policy choice.

Rule retained across this whole map — the 30-year stayed high after the high-yield auction because of curve uninversion / front-end steepening and term premium, not a ratings cut, a "too much debt" headline, or a CPI print. Hunt's scorecard: nominal rate = real rate + expected inflation + term/risk premium — and all three can rise together, which is exactly what makes this thread and the fiscal-dominance thread inseparable in practice.

Investment strategy & portfolio

This is Sleeve A (Liquidity) — the sleeve whose entire job is to survive Clock A and stay positioned to ride the bill rail if the neo-mercantilist alignment thread scales stablecoin demand for short paper. Default posture: bills, cash-like instruments, and pre-arranged swap or repo access, arranged before a stress event, not during one.

Do

Hold liquidity in bills; keep swap/repo lines pre-arranged; treat any single buyback rally as unproven until it survives more than a week.

Don't

Rebuild core 20–30y duration on a bid that hasn't demonstrated durability — that's a tactical call for Sleeve C (Duration), not a core allocation, and Sleeve C stays small until its own clock actually prints.

What this means to trade

Individuals

Shorten personal duration. Hold near-term needs in bills, not bonds. Don't chase a 30-year rally that hasn't survived a full week yet. Build dollar liquidity ahead of a funding-stress event, not after one.

Companies

Term out debt ahead of the refinancing wall. Match asset life to liability life. Buy USD liquidity before Clock A stress hits. Use options and collars instead of a naked bet on official support holding.

Nation-states

Shorten official portfolio duration toward 0–2y paper. Pre-arrange dollar swap and repo lines now, while they're cheap to set up. Don't assume a headline buyback ceiling permanently pins the long end — it didn't survive a week in August.
