The store-of-value job is moving out of the bond market.

Not toward one clean successor — toward a coexistence. Official gold and dollar-denominated bills or stablecoins appear to be splitting the work that a 30-year Treasury used to do alone: one holds value, the other keeps settlement moving.

Where it's playing out

China

The four large state banks shut retail paper-gold products; Shanghai Gold Exchange physical delivery and Hong Kong vault capacity are both expanding (Bravos Research, 27 Aug). China's Treasury holdings are down roughly 50% since 2014 — a slow, multi-year rotation away from long coupons, not a sudden move. June's roughly 173-tonne import figure against an approximately $125bn trade surplus implies settlement-scale gold pricing well above the paper market — Gromen's range runs $22,600–$25,700.

Europe / UK

Gold has already displaced the euro as the second official reserve asset (Lacalle, 27 Aug) — a threshold that appears to have been crossed quietly, without a single dramatic headline marking the moment.

Global

IMF COFER data — which covers FX reserves only, and explicitly excludes gold — still shows the US dollar around 56–57% of FX reserves even as the gold side of the overall reserve picture shifts underneath it. The two data sets are answering different questions, and reading them together is where the real signal is.

How the analysts read it

Daniel Lacalle

Three limits are already broken — economic, fiscal, inflationary. Long sovereign debt is losing the store-of-value job specifically, not the transaction job. The dollar stays the fiat reserve currency simply because every other fiat option is worse. Stablecoins are the bridge letting the transaction dollar keep functioning even as the store-of-value job migrates elsewhere. CBDCs, in his read, are recentralization dressed as innovation — not a hedge against anything.

Luke Gromen

Settlement-scale gold arithmetic built from China's import-versus-surplus gap. A gold-certificate revaluation could inject roughly $5–5.5tn into the TGA — presented explicitly as arithmetic, not a week-one prediction.

Bravos Research

The China paper-gold shutdown is a plumbing story as much as a price story — retail flow is being redirected specifically toward physical delivery and Hong Kong vaulting.

Brent Johnson

Reframes "Fort Knox" as a reserve-policy question rather than a conspiracy question — the live issue is how official gold gets valued and used, not whether it physically exists.

Crowded-trade fix to hold onto — a gold ETF is not allocated metal, and BTC is not official gold. Two different vehicles doing two different jobs; conflating them is the single most common error on this thread.

What this means to trade

Individuals

Size allocated gold so a 20–30% Clock A drawdown doesn't force a sale at the worst moment. Don't assume gold offsets a stronger dollar and a higher 30-year printing on the same day — historically it often doesn't.

Companies

Hold working capital in bills rather than reaching for a stablecoin yield story prematurely — the rail is a settlement innovation here, not yet a proven treasury-management tool at scale.

Nation-states

Accumulate official gold specifically on DXY or real-yield spikes, not steadily regardless of price. Keep allocated gold in a jurisdiction you can actually deliver from, not just custody in. Treat a CBDC rollout as a control-architecture decision, not a reserve-diversification one.
