Where it's playing out

United States

Entitlements, interest, and veterans spending combined run near 105% of federal receipts (Gromen). Debt is moving from roughly 120% toward 130% of GDP, and interest expense already exceeds military spending (Hunt). Layered on top: AI capex on a path Gammon cites as roughly $400bn (2025) → $800bn (2026) → over $1tn (2027) → $1.2tn by 2029. The GDPNow slump and the cancelled Blackstone data-center campus already showed this capex hitting the import/deficit channel back in July. Gromen's 26 August note is the sharpest transmission claim here: healthcare-administration job cuts, if used to close the fiscal gap before the balance sheet itself is restructured, flow directly into mortgage, auto, and card delinquencies.

How the analysts read it

Luke Gromen

NIIP near −80% of GDP. A soft form of yield-curve control is already underway. AI capex near 3.5% of GDP is, in this read, a fiscal-dominance multiplier — not simply a private investment story anymore.

Lacy Hunt

A genuinely new inflationary era: capital scarcity, net national savings near zero, potential growth capped around 2–2.5%. All three Fisher terms — real rate, expected inflation, term premium — can rise together, a materially different regime from the 2010s.

George Gammon

Two separate reserve clocks run at once: a holdings-based one (~5 years) and a transaction-dollar one (≥25 years). The TGA is the next tool after buybacks, not a hypothetical one. The S&P–housing–economy pyramid means an AI-capex-driven equity move now carries more systemic reach than it used to.

Where this connects to Clock A — Hunt's scorecard (nominal rate = real rate + expected inflation + term/risk premium) is the exact mechanism linking this thread to dollar-funding stress. A cooler CPI print combined with large deficits is a trap, not a green light — it confirms Snider's shock-fade and Hunt's broken-savings read simultaneously.

Investment strategy & portfolio

This is where Sleeve D (Real Capacity) earns its keep — not as a growth bet on AI itself, but as the accumulation list for after a capex-driven credit event, not during the melt-up. For nation-states, the discipline is specific: don't treat "waste" or healthcare-admin cuts as a free fiscal fix — restructure the actual balance sheet before counting on job cuts to close the gap.

Companies (esp. hyperscalers)

Treat the roughly $500bn third-party AI financing architecture as off-balance-sheet duration. Hedge your own and peer credit spreads. Stage campuses — the Blackstone cancellation is the tell for what happens when you don't.

Sleeve E — Credit hedge

Spreads, private credit, consumer delinquency. Listed CDS/basis positions and haircut private-credit NAVs rather than waiting for a marked event.

Milestones on this thread

Milestone 0 — already printed (late Aug 2026)

Wide hyperscaler credit spreads; the Gromen 26 Aug note on healthcare-admin cuts as a default-transmission channel. Inventory AI exposure by free cash flow now, not later.

Milestone 2 — 1 to 3 months out

This is where the thread gets tested hardest: 2027 capex guides land, and consumer delinquency data starts to run against AI job-cut data. Capex up + FCF down = credit event, by this map's own definition.

Milestone 3 — 2027

Capex approaches the $1tn mark, or the sector hits its first real winter. If the sovereign side is pinned but corporates aren't, the corporate-to-Treasury credit basis becomes the trade — and the post-washout Sleeve D accumulation list opens, not before.

What this means to trade

Individuals

Trim the most negative-FCF AI exposure first, before a forced rebalancing does it for you. Treat human-capital skills that pair with AI (rather than compete with the healthcare-admin-cut channel) as the personal-level hedge.

Companies

Haircut private-credit lines and NAVs now, don't wait for a marked event. Hold working capital in bills, not commercial paper that assumes the credit cycle stays benign.

Nation-states

Watch NIIP and interest-to-receipts before any other fiscal headline. A "cut waste" narrative without an actual balance-sheet restructuring plan should be read as a stalling tactic under this framework, not a solution.
