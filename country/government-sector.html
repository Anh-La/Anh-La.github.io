2. Key Fiscal Balances & Revenue Forecasting

The Core Balance Metrics

  1. Net Operating Balance (NOB): Revenue − Expense. The current-period flow surplus or deficit before capital spending.
  2. Net Lending / Net Borrowing (NLB): NOB − NANA. The government's overall financing need, where NANA is the Net Acquisition of Nonfinancial Assets.
  3. Primary Fiscal Balance: Overall Balance + Net Interest Expenditure. Strips out the legacy interest bill to isolate the current policy stance.
  4. Structural Fiscal Balance: Adjusts the total balance for output fluctuations, isolating the discretionary fiscal stance from the automatic effects of the business cycle.

Practical Example: Forecasting Revenue & Debt Feedback Loops

⚠Worked Scenario

Revenue Forecast: Tax revenue is projected using tax elasticity (εT): ΔTax Revenue = Tax Revenuet−1 × εT × ΔNominal GDP.

Debt Feedback: When NLB < 0, the government issues debt (ΔD), and interest expense follows Interestt = r × Dt−1. Higher interest expense worsens the following period's operating balance, which in turn raises the next period's borrowing requirement — an iterative loop that projection models must resolve period by period.

3. Structural Linkages & Sector Specific Glossary

Fiscal Transmission Channels

  • Fiscal → Monetary Link: Fiscal deficits require net financing from central/commercial banks, expanding Net Claims on Government (ΔNCG) and driving broad money growth.
  • Fiscal → External Link: Public external debt issuance directly finances foreign balance of payments gaps.

Sector Glossary & Metrics

Automatic Stabilizers
Revenue/expense mechanisms that expand or contract naturally with output cycles, without policy intervention.
Tax Elasticity ($\epsilon_T$)
Proportional change in tax collections relative to changes in nominal GDP.
